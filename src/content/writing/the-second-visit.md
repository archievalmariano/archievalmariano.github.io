The first time a reader lands on your site, they usually didn't mean to. A link,
a search, a share — arrival is mostly circumstance. The second visit is
different. The second visit is a choice.

That's why I've come to think of return as the most honest metric a publication
has. It can't be manufactured by a good headline alone. It reflects something
closer to trust: the reader deciding, however small the decision, that you were
worth coming back to.

## Building around return

When a newsroom starts measuring return seriously, its priorities shift. The
question changes from _how do we get seen_ to _what would make someone come
back_. Those are very different editorial instincts, and the second one tends to
produce better journalism as a side effect.

None of this makes reach unimportant. It makes reach insufficient. The work is
to convert the accident of the first visit into the decision of the second — and
then to keep earning it.
