Attention is cheap and audience is expensive, and much of the trouble in
digital publishing comes from treating them as the same thing.

Attention is a spike. Someone arrives, reads, and leaves, and whether they ever
return is left to chance and the algorithm. Audience is a relationship. It
implies return, recognition, and some measure of trust — a reader who comes
back not because a feed served them but because they chose to.

## Why the confusion persists

The metrics we inherited measure attention beautifully and audience barely at
all. Pageviews, sessions, unique visitors: all snapshots of arrival, none of
them evidence of return. So we optimize what we can see, and we end up with a lot
of attention and very little audience.

## What building audience actually asks

It asks for patience, for a consistent editorial promise, and for measurement
that privileges frequency and loyalty over volume. It asks the newsroom to care
about the second visit more than the first.

None of this is novel. But it's remarkable how much strategy still quietly
assumes that enough attention will eventually add up to an audience. It won't.
Audience is built on purpose, or not at all.
