<p>{`This is not a review.`}</p>

<p>{`If you're looking for a write-up on the specs and capabilities of this camera, this is the wrong content for you. Pretty sure much has already been written (and shot!) about this incredibly popular camera. I would know, I was on a waitlist before I got this, one year into release.`}</p>

<p>{`But I'm getting ahead of myself.`}</p>

<p>{`In fandom parlance, OTP means one true pairing, as in the one pairing that fans swear by. It doesn't necessarily have to be "canon," or what actually happened in the story. Some follow canon to an, admittedly, unhealthy degree. So fun. So nerdy. And a whole lot of cringe.`}</p>

<p>{`Thing is, an OTP isn't necessarily the "best" or even the most logical pairing. It's just the best for you. That can involve a lot of suspension of disbelief, leaps of faith and/or hope, and some pretty extreme opinions.`}</p>

<p>{`That gets us back to this, admittedly, very beautiful camera. Easily the most beautiful hardware I have ever owned, and I daily-ed an iPhone Air, mind you. Despite the insane demand (and good effing lord, I just checked and SRP jumped by PHP 10,000... two years into release), the X100VI can be polarizing. Fuji cameras are notorious for their slow autofocus, especially compared to cameras at the same price point. There's also the "Fuji look," a more filmic look for its fans and photos going through the piss filter for those who aren't.`}</p>

<p>{`But I didn't care. I wanted it.`}</p>

<Figure src={woodenHand} alt="A black Fujifilm X100VI held upright by an articulated wooden hand." orientation="portrait" widths={[360, 600, 900, 1200]} />

<p>{`I already had experience with Fuji cameras and had decided I could live with their limitations and flaws. As long as I never had to even look at Lightroom again. I still save my shots in both JPEG and RAW, of course. I am not that... brave. Yup, let's go with brave.`}</p>

<p>{`There's also the film simulations and the numerous recipes available online. I've gone through the whole arc, saving a lot of those recipes before eventually defaulting to Classic Neg most of the time. Fuji cameras just make me want to take a photo. Fiddle with the analog controls, feel like a pro for all 10 seconds. Screw on lens filters to make the photos feel less digital and more film, the irony not lost on me, sure, but like all Fuji shooters, we didn't care.`}</p>

<p>{`And there I was at the camera store, having finally received the call that morning after a couple of months of waiting. The only problem was that they only had the black one, which took all three seconds for me on the phone to decide that I was taking it.`}</p>

<Figure src={wildflour} alt="A hand holding the black X100VI over a table set with plates at Wildflour." orientation="portrait" widths={[360, 600, 900, 1200]} />

<p>{`The size was perfect and the camera itself was beautiful. I couldn't think of anything else while I held it, thinking that black wasn't so bad after all. And did I mention that it was beautiful? So beautiful that I could forget, for the moment, the ridiculous price tag (straight payment only, with bundled accessories that pushed up the SRP). Of course, that didn't end there. I bought a black pro mist filter from Urth and a "retro" cage from SmallRig that might as well double the weight and footprint of the X100VI. I later replaced the cage with a smaller, lighter grip.`}</p>

<Figure src={cage} alt="A hand holding the X100VI fitted with a metal SmallRig cage and a brown grip." orientation="portrait" widths={[360, 600, 900, 1200]} />

<p>{`Thing is, I already had two cameras at that time, being afflicted with gear acquisition syndrome (GAS). An X-T30 II and an X-T5. Both Fuji, the latter more capable, of course. Also had a couple of lenses. Did I mention a bad bout of GAS? A TTArtisan 27mm pancake lens, which, when attached to the X-T30 II, meant I could pretend it was an X100VI, plus a 16mm, a 33mm, and a 56mm. The last one, perhaps my favorite and the heaviest of the bunch. I thought I could have a system wherein I could use all of them. And indeed, for a time, I did.`}</p>

<p>{`The first to go was the X-T30 II.`}</p>

<p>{`It wasn't a cheap camera, but compared to the X100VI, it might as well have been a toy. The other lenses followed, and I was left with the X-T5 and the 56mm f/1.2. I brought both cameras on a week-long trip to Singapore. It was a chore bringing both systems, but the shots were breathtaking.`}</p>

<p>{`There I was tracing Esplanade Bridge, in some sort of ridiculous pilgrimage through Singapore in the small hours of the morning, camera and heart in hand. Some days I would go from Clarke Quay to Marina Bay, some toward the other direction, Chinatown. There was something about a cosmopolitan city like Singapore becoming as quiet as it was during those hours, not still but more like humming. It was surreal being the only person at an otherwise busy intersection and still following traffic rules. I would sometimes use the seconds I had to wait for the green light to take slow-shutter shots of cars passing through. There were some good shots, and then I wouldn't have the patience to wait for another round of cars and would just continue on my way. I would pass by mostly empty hawker centers, with some shops already preparing for the day.`}</p>

<Figure src={nightRoad} alt="A nearly empty Singapore street at night, lit by streetlamps." orientation="landscape" />

<p>{`Some days I took the X-T5, some the X100VI. More often the latter, particularly when I wanted to do slow-shutter photography. The built-in ND filter was a godsend. However, it couldn't match the deadly combo of the X-T5 and the 56mm, particularly for portraits. Though the X-T5 was a camera-camera, meaning you could barely forget that you had it with you. Compared to truly small cameras, the X100VI is not pocketable at all. But there's something about its size that makes it more handy. Or was I just comparing it to the massive one I also had with me back then?`}</p>

<Figure src={traffic} alt="Blurred cars passing a lit building at a Singapore intersection at night." orientation="landscape" />

<p>{`So one day, I just decided to sell the X-T5 and the 56mm. Seeing them always in the dry cabinet, wanting to take them but finding them almost impossibly large and heavy. Succeeding trips, too, saw me just bring the X100VI. I figured that I was doing them a disservice by keeping them and that someone, another Fuji fan at least, would be able to use them and make the most out of them.`}</p>

<p>{`And so that leaves me with just one camera. Or perhaps three? I found myself using the digital teleconverter a lot, switching from the usual 35mm-equivalent view to 50mm and sometimes 70mm. I briefly toyed with the idea of buying the teleconverter lens but couldn't justify the price and the heft it would add, and thought that the digital one would suffice for me.`}</p>

<p>{`Sure, there are times when I'm certain that the X-T5 with a different lens, not necessarily the 56mm, could have taken a better shot. But it was more a recognition of that camera than a longing for it back. I think I have arrived, after an embarrassing amount of time and money spent, at a point in my life wherein I value having to make fewer decisions. Get the sling bag, take the camera, and just shoot. Of course, I still fiddle with the film simulations and recipes from time to time, but most of the time, I just settle with Classic Neg and a prayer.`}</p>

<p>{`Will the X100VI remain my favorite camera? Perhaps, perhaps not. This hobby can be as slow-paced as one would want, I discovered. The best cameras and lenses? Every brand seems to have new ones every year or two, including Fuji. But for the vast majority of casual users such as myself, upgrading every couple of years seems increasingly unnecessary. Knowing that and actually feeling it are different things. An expensive, a very expensive lesson, but I'll take it.`}</p>

<p>{`Besides, one doesn't ditch an OTP that easily. You hold on to it and keep choosing it even in the presence of new ones, in the presence of canon. Or Sony.`}</p>
