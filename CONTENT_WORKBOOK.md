# Portfolio content workbook

This file separates Archieval's authorship from editorial and structural support.
The current site copy remains a working draft until each section is reviewed.

## Editing agreement

For personal and introductory writing, Archieval writes the first draft. Editing should:

- preserve vocabulary, rhythm, humor, and point of view;
- correct friction and repetition without making the writing sound corporate;
- flag structural suggestions separately from line edits;
- retain an original draft beside any substantial revision;
- never add personal claims, motivations, or memories that Archieval did not provide.

For work-related writing, structure can be proposed first. Claims must remain attributable to
the supplied project material, and missing outcomes must stay missing rather than being inferred.

## Homepage prompts

### Cover note

Aim for 80–140 words. The current headline can stay, change, or become a smaller deck.

- What do you find yourself helping people understand repeatedly?
- What kind of messy problem makes you want to start mapping things out?
- What connects writing, audience analytics, e-commerce, and operational design for you?
- What would you like a stranger to understand about your work within thirty seconds?

### The throughline

Aim for two or three short paragraphs, not a career chronology.

- Which questions followed you from editorial work into strategy and analytics?
- What did being a writer/editor teach you that still affects the way you build systems?
- What does "useful" work mean to you?

### Homepage closing

Aim for 60–100 words.

- What kind of conversation or opportunity would you genuinely like this site to begin?
- What is something your résumé cannot adequately communicate?

## About prompts

### Opening

Aim for 100–160 words.

- How would you introduce yourself without reciting job titles?
- Where are you based, and does that location shape how you see digital work?
- What are you curious about outside a formal role?

### The longer version

Aim for 500–800 words. This can be written as several titled fragments rather than one essay.

- What did you think your career would be when you began writing and editing?
- Which transitions felt deliberate, and which happened because the work changed around you?
- What did publishing, branded content, client work, e-commerce, and analytics each teach you?
- Which parts of your background would never appear in a conventional professional biography?

### How I work

Aim for 250–400 words.

- What conditions help you do your best work?
- What do you distrust in strategy, analytics, or automation?
- What should remain a human judgment even when a process can be automated?
- What does a maintainable system look like to you?

## Work case-study worksheet

Fragments and bullet points are enough for the first pass.

1. **The situation** — What was already happening? Who experienced the problem?
2. **What I noticed** — What pattern, constraint, or missed opportunity became clear?
3. **My responsibility** — What did you own, influence, or advise? What belonged to others?
4. **What I did** — What decisions, artifacts, systems, or processes did you create?
5. **Evidence** — What can be shown: figures, screenshots, documents, diagrams, or published work?
6. **What became possible** — What changed? If an outcome is unknown, say what capability existed afterward.
7. **What I learned** — What would you repeat, change, or question now?
8. **Boundaries** — What is confidential, approximate, reconstructed, or outside your authorship?

## Visual evidence checklist

- original screenshots or safe crops;
- reports, decks, scripts, spreadsheets, or planning documents;
- published pages and credited editorial work;
- diagrams that can be reconstructed from the actual process;
- verified figures suitable for large typographic treatment;
- captions explaining what the artifact proves and what it does not prove.
